most Shopify stores don't know where they're losing sales

Published ecommerce conversion benchmarks land somewhere between 1 and 3%, depending on who is measuring. The gap between that and what a store could do is rarely a mystery once someone looks: a checkout step people abandon, a product page that doesn't answer the one question shoppers have, or tracking that doesn't match what's really happening in the store.

Finding it takes structured testing and real data analysis. A redesign based on a hunch usually moves the numbers in the wrong direction.

who's behind this: ecomloop is Andrew Maury, a data scientist who has built and optimized Shopify stores since 2018, including his own apparel brand. One person, no account managers, no handoffs.

🔍 what I do

A/B testing done properly

Structured tests on the pages that matter most: product pages, cart, checkout. Sample sizes and significance thresholds are set before the test starts, so the result means something.

Tracking and analytics audits

Many stores have a gap between what their analytics report and what Shopify shows in revenue. I find the gap and fix the tracking so decisions rest on real numbers.

Customer research and data analysis

Before testing anything, I look at what customers do: survey data, funnel drop-off, session behavior. Every test targets a problem visible in the data.
