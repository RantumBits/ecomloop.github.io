your Google Ads may be bidding on orders that never happened

tracking reconciliation for distributors and suppliers who sell online, by phone and through branches

why distributors are prone to this

Smart Bidding learns from the conversions your website reports. When two Google Ads tags count the same order, or a tag from an old account is still counting next to the current one, Google sees more conversions than you had and raises your bids to buy more of them. You pay that premium on every auction until someone looks.

Distributors and suppliers are set up for this to go unnoticed. Most of the revenue closes by phone, at a branch or on a trade account, so the website is a small slice that nobody audits. The agency that installed the tags has often moved on. And the conversion that matters is frequently a quote request rather than a checkout, which is easy to count twice and hard to check against anything.

🔎 what a scan of 17 supplier sites found

October 2026, run from the outside, no access

I ran the scanner on 20 distributor and supplier sites across industrial and safety supply, dental, veterinary, uniforms and replacement parts. The scan completed on 17, and none of them were on Shopify.

  • 10 of 17 had at least one confirmed tracking defect.
  • 5 of 17 loaded more than one Google Ads ID on the same site, the pattern that double-counts or misfiles conversions.
  • 3 of 17 still loaded a Universal Analytics tag. Google switched UA off in July 2023, so those tags have collected nothing for three years.

Confirmed means the IDs are in the page source where anyone can see them. Whether a given duplicate is costing money depends on whether you spend on that platform, which is the first thing the audit settles.
